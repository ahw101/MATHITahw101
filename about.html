Why this site exists

Most free maths worksheets online are PDFs. You download one, your child does it, and the next day you need another — except the only other one at the right level is behind a sign-up form, or it is the same sheet with the numbers moved around.

Math It! generates a new sheet every time you press the button. The questions are built in your browser from the year group and difficulty you choose, marked instantly, and printed on exactly one side of A4. There is no account, no limit, and no point at which it asks for money.

Around that generator we have written 41 topic guides, 11 times table pages and 5 year-group overviews — because the sheet is only half of it. When a child gets stuck on short division, what you need is not another worksheet, it is someone explaining where the method goes wrong.

Who writes it

Math It! is made by a small independent team with classroom and one-to-one tutoring experience in UK primary maths, working with an editor who has no maths background — which turns out to be the most useful person in the process. If an explanation does not survive contact with someone who has forgotten how long division works, it goes back for a rewrite.

We are not a publisher, a tuition company or a lead generator for one. Nothing on this site is sponsored, and we do not accept paid links, guest posts or product placements in the guides.

Editorial standards

Four rules that decide what goes on a page and what gets cut.

Written for one reader at a time

Every guide is written for a specific person: a parent at a kitchen table at seven in the evening, trying to help with homework they last did thirty years ago. That constraint decides the structure — method first, worked examples second, the mistakes we see most often third.

Checked against the statutory curriculum

Year-group expectations are taken from the England national curriculum programmes of study for mathematics, key stages 1 and 2. We paraphrase rather than quote, and we say which year an objective belongs to so you can tell whether something is early, on time or overdue.

Arithmetic verified, not assumed

Every worked example on this site has had its arithmetic checked independently of the text it sits in. Generated worksheet questions are produced and marked by the same code, so a question and its answer cannot disagree.

No filler, no spinning, no scraping

Nothing here is rewritten from another site, assembled from scraped material, or padded to hit a word count. Where two pages cover related ground — the 6 and 12 times tables, say — they say different things, because the useful facts about them genuinely differ.

How a page gets made

The same five steps for every guide, every table page and every year overview.

  1. 1

    Scope

    We decide what the page is for and who needs it, usually from a real question a parent or teacher has asked us.

  2. 2

    Draft

    A subject specialist drafts the method, the worked examples and the misconceptions. AI tools may help with structure, phrasing and first drafts.

  3. 3

    Edit

    Every draft is rewritten by a human editor. In practice very little of a first draft survives: the examples get replaced with better ones, the explanations get shorter, and anything vague gets cut.

  4. 4

    Fact-check

    Arithmetic is recomputed. Curriculum claims are checked against the programmes of study. Anything we cannot verify is removed rather than hedged.

  5. 5

    Publish and revisit

    Pages are reviewed when the curriculum changes, when we spot an error, or when a reader tells us something is unclear.

Methodology

How the questions are generated

Every question is built in your browser by a generator we wrote, not pulled from a bank of pre-written sums. When you choose a year group and a difficulty, the generator does four things:

  1. Picks a topic from the set appropriate to that year — 34 topics are available, from number bonds to ratio and simple algebra.
  2. Chooses the numbers inside the range set for that year and level, with rules that keep the question sensible: no division that does not resolve, no subtraction that goes negative unless negatives are the topic.
  3. Computes the answer at the same moment it builds the question, from the same values, so the two cannot drift apart.
  4. Renders the question and keeps the answer aside until you ask to mark the sheet.

Sheets are identified by a seed number, printed in the header. Enter the same seed and you get the same sheet back — useful for a class set, or for marking a sheet a week later. Everything happens on your device; no answers are sent anywhere.

Questions about this site

Who writes Math It!?
A small team with classroom and tutoring experience in UK primary maths, working with a qualified editor. We are not a publisher or a tuition company, and nothing here is sponsored content.
Do you use AI to write the content?
AI tools are used in drafting, in the same way a word processor or a style guide is used. No page is published as a machine draft: everything is rewritten, fact-checked and signed off by a person, and the worked examples and misconceptions come from real teaching experience.
How are the worksheet questions generated?
By an open piece of JavaScript running in your browser. It picks a topic, builds a question inside the number ranges set for that year and difficulty, and computes the answer at the same moment. Nothing is drawn from a question bank, so sheets are never recycled.
Is the content copied from anywhere else?
No. Every explanation, example and FAQ on this site was written for it. The national curriculum objectives are paraphrased from the Department for Education's published programmes of study, which is a public document, and we say so on the pages where we use it.
How do you make money?
Advertising, through Google AdSense. There is no subscription, no paywall and no email capture. Advertisers have no influence over what we publish, and we do not accept paid links or guest posts.
What if you have made a mistake?
Tell us and we will fix it. Email hello@mathit.co.uk with the page and what is wrong. Corrections to maths errors are made the same week we can verify them.
Found a problem?

Tell us and we will fix it

A wrong answer, an explanation that does not land, a question pitched at the wrong year — all of it is worth reporting. Corrections are made as soon as we can verify them.
