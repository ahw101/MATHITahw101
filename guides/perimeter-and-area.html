Perimeter and area, and how to stop confusing them

Years 3–6 6 min read Practise this →

Perimeter is the distance all the way round the outside of a shape. Area is the amount of flat space inside it. They are measured in different units and they answer different questions, yet they are confused constantly — largely because they are taught in the same week.

The fix is a concrete pair of images: perimeter is the fence, area is the grass.

The method, step by step

  1. 1

    Perimeter: add every side

    Walk round the shape and total the lengths. For a rectangle that is 2 × (length + width); for a regular shape it is one side × the number of sides.

  2. 2

    Area of a rectangle: length × width

    Because the shape is a grid: 8 rows of 5 squares is 40 squares.

  3. 3

    Area of a triangle: base × height ÷ 2

    Any triangle is exactly half of a rectangle drawn around it. The height must be the perpendicular height — straight up from the base — not a slanted side.

  4. 4

    Area of a parallelogram: base × perpendicular height

    Slice the triangle off one end and slide it to the other and you have a rectangle. Again, not the sloping side.

  5. 5

    Compound shapes: cut into rectangles

    Split an L-shape into two rectangles, find each area, then add. Or box the whole thing in and subtract the missing piece — whichever gives easier numbers.

  6. 6

    Use the right units

    Perimeter is a length, so cm or m. Area is squares, so cm² or m². Marks are routinely lost for the unit alone.

Worked examples

Where it usually goes wrong

✕ Using a sloping side as the height of a triangle.

✓ The height must be perpendicular to the base — straight up from it. On a diagram it is the dashed line, not the slanted edge.

✕ Forgetting to halve when finding a triangle's area.

✓ A triangle is half its surrounding rectangle. If you forget, your answer is exactly double.

✕ Giving an area in cm instead of cm².

✓ Area counts squares. The unit is always squared, and omitting it loses the mark.

✕ Assuming two shapes with the same perimeter have the same area.

✓ They rarely do. A 1 × 11 rectangle and a 6 × 6 square both have a perimeter of 24, but areas of 11 and 36.

Why area uses squared units

Area is measured by counting how many 1 cm squares fit inside. A rectangle 12 cm by 7 cm fits 12 squares along each of 7 rows, which is 84 squares — 84 cm².

That also explains a conversion that catches everybody: 1 m = 100 cm, but 1 m² = 10,000 cm², because a square metre is 100 cm by 100 cm.

The same perimeter, very different areas

Give children 24 cm of string and ask for the biggest possible rectangle. They will usually try long thin ones first:

  • 1 × 11 → area 11 cm²
  • 3 × 9 → area 27 cm²
  • 5 × 7 → area 35 cm²
  • 6 × 6 → area 36 cm²

The square always wins. It is a genuinely surprising result at age ten and it kills the “perimeter and area are the same thing” misconception for good.

Missing-side problems

A standard Year 6 question gives the area and one side and asks for the other, or gives the perimeter and asks for a side. Both are inverse operations.

Area 84 cm² with one side 12 cm → the other side is 84 ÷ 12 = 7 cm. Perimeter 38 cm with one side 12 cm → half the perimeter is 19 cm, so the other side is 19 − 12 = 7 cm. Halving the perimeter first is the step children miss.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between perimeter and area?
Perimeter is the distance around the outside, measured in cm or m. Area is the space inside, measured in cm² or m². Fence versus grass.
What is the formula for the area of a triangle?
Base × perpendicular height ÷ 2. The height must be measured at right angles to the base.
How do you find the area of an L-shape?
Split it into two rectangles and add, or box it into one big rectangle and subtract the missing piece.
Why is 1 m² not 100 cm²?
Because a square metre is 100 cm along both sides, so it holds 100 × 100 = 10,000 square centimetres.
