Angles: measuring, estimating and calculating

Years 3–6 6 min read Practise this →

An angle measures turn, not length. Two lines meeting at 40° make the same angle whether they are 2 cm or 2 metres long — a point children find genuinely counter-intuitive the first time they meet a diagram drawn out of scale.

Angles are measured in degrees. A full turn is 360°, a quarter turn is the right angle at 90°, and almost every angle question at primary is solved by one of four facts.

The method, step by step

  1. 1

    Learn the four names

    Acute is less than 90°. Right is exactly 90°. Obtuse is between 90° and 180°. Reflex is more than 180°. A straight line is 180°.

  2. 2

    Estimate before you measure

    Decide whether the angle is bigger or smaller than a right angle first. That tells you which protractor scale to read and catches the commonest error before it happens.

  3. 3

    Line the protractor up properly

    Centre hole exactly on the vertex, zero line exactly along one arm. If either is out, every reading afterwards is wrong.

  4. 4

    Read the scale that starts at zero

    A protractor has two scales running opposite ways. Follow the one where your starting arm reads 0, not 180.

  5. 5

    Use the angle facts instead of measuring

    Angles on a straight line total 180°. Angles round a point total 360°. Angles in a triangle total 180°. Angles in a quadrilateral total 360°. Most exam questions are solved by subtraction from one of these.

Where it usually goes wrong

✕ Reading the wrong protractor scale, giving 133° instead of 47°.

✓ Estimate first. If the angle is clearly less than a right angle, an answer above 90° is wrong — you read the outer scale instead of the inner one.

✕ Thinking longer arms make a bigger angle.

✓ An angle measures turn only. Extending the arms changes nothing.

✕ Assuming a diagram is drawn to scale.

✓ Unless it says so, it is not. Use the given numbers and the angle facts, never a ruler or a protractor on a printed exam diagram.

✕ Forgetting that a reflex angle and its partner add to 360°.

✓ If the inner angle is 110°, the reflex angle outside it is 360 − 110 = 250°.

The four facts that solve almost everything

  • Angles on a straight line add to 180°.
  • Angles round a point add to 360°.
  • Angles in a triangle add to 180°.
  • Angles in a quadrilateral add to 360°.

Vertically opposite angles — the pair across an X crossing — are equal, which follows from the straight-line fact. Between them these five cover the whole of KS2 and most of early KS3.

Why a triangle is always 180°

Tear the three corners off a paper triangle and place them edge to edge. They always form a straight line. Any triangle, every time.

It is one of the few primary facts with a demonstration a child can do themselves in thirty seconds, and it sticks far better than being told. A quadrilateral is 360° for a related reason: any quadrilateral splits into two triangles.

Angles and turns

Angle also describes rotation, which is where it meets compass directions and clock faces:

  • Quarter turn = 90°, half turn = 180°, three-quarter turn = 270°, full turn = 360°.
  • A clock's minute hand turns 360° in an hour, so 6° a minute.
  • The hour hand turns 30° an hour — the gap between two numbers.

“What is the angle between the hands at 3 o'clock?” is 90°, and it is a favourite reasoning question precisely because it joins two topics.

Questions people ask

What are the types of angle?
Acute (under 90°), right (exactly 90°), obtuse (90° to 180°), straight (180°) and reflex (over 180°).
How do I stop reading the wrong protractor scale?
Estimate whether the angle is acute or obtuse before you read it, and always follow the scale on which your starting arm reads zero.
Do angles in every triangle add to 180 degrees?
Yes, for every flat triangle, whatever its shape or size.
When do children learn to use a protractor?
Year 5 in England — drawing and measuring angles to the nearest degree. Year 4 compares and orders angles without measuring.
