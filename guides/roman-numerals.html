Number & place value

Roman numerals

Years 3–6 5 min read Practise this →

Roman numerals sit slightly apart from the rest of primary maths. There is no arithmetic to do — the whole topic is reading and writing a different notation, which makes it a rare chance for children who find calculation hard to shine.

The English curriculum introduces numerals to 12 in Year 3 (via the clock), to 100 in Year 4, and to 1000 with years such as MCMXCIV in Year 5.

The method, step by step

  1. 1

    Learn the seven letters

    I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500, M = 1000. Everything else is built from these. A common mnemonic for the order is I Value Xylophones Like Cows Dig Milk.

  2. 2

    Bigger before smaller means add

    Read left to right and total them up. VI = 5 + 1 = 6. XXVII = 10 + 10 + 5 + 1 + 1 = 27.

  3. 3

    Smaller before bigger means subtract

    IV = 5 − 1 = 4. IX = 10 − 1 = 9. XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400, CM = 900. Only I, X and C are ever used this way.

  4. 4

    Never repeat a letter more than three times

    III is 3, but 4 is IV, not IIII. Likewise XXX is 30 and 40 is XL. V, L and D are never repeated at all.

  5. 5

    Build big numbers thousands first

    Work down through M, then hundreds, then tens, then units. 1994 → 1000 (M) + 900 (CM) + 90 (XC) + 4 (IV) = MCMXCIV.

Worked examples

Where it usually goes wrong

✕ Writing 4 as IIII and 9 as VIIII.

✓ No letter repeats more than three times. 4 is IV and 9 is IX.

✕ Inventing subtraction pairs such as IC for 99.

✓ Only I before V or X, X before L or C, and C before D or M are allowed. 99 is XCIX.

✕ Reading IX as 11.

✓ The smaller letter comes first, so it subtracts: 10 − 1 = 9. XI is 11.

✕ Building a number out of order.

✓ Always go from the largest value down. 1500 is MD, never DM.

Why clock faces use IIII

Plenty of clocks show IIII rather than IV at four o'clock. It is a real convention — the usual explanations are visual balance against the VIII opposite, and the practicalities of casting numerals from a fixed set of letters.

It is a nice bit of history, and worth telling children explicitly that it is not what to write in a test. Exams expect IV.

Why the Romans never needed zero

Roman numerals have no symbol for zero and no place value. The letter C means a hundred wherever it appears, which is why the system is fine for recording numbers and hopeless for calculating with them — try long multiplication in numerals and the problem becomes obvious within a minute.

That contrast is the real educational value of the topic. Our place value guide covers the system that replaced it, where a digit's position carries the meaning.

Questions people ask

What are the seven Roman numerals?
I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500 and M = 1000.
Why is 4 written as IV and not IIII?
No letter repeats more than three times, so 4 is written as one less than five. Clock faces often use IIII for visual balance, but exams expect IV.
How do you write 2026 in Roman numerals?
MMXXVI — that is MM for 2000, XX for 20 and VI for 6.
When do children learn Roman numerals?
Year 3 to 12 for clock faces, Year 4 to 100, and Year 5 to 1000 including years.
