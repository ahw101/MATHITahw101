Volume and capacity

Years 5–6 5 min read Practise this →

Volume is how much three-dimensional space something takes up. Capacity is how much a container can hold. In practice the numbers are often the same, and at primary level the two words are used almost interchangeably — but the distinction is worth knowing: a brick has volume, a bucket has capacity.

Volume is measured in cubic units, cm³ or m³. Capacity is usually measured in millilitres and litres. The two systems join at a neat point: 1 ml = 1 cm³ exactly.

The method, step by step

  1. 1

    Start by counting cubes

    Before any formula, build or draw a cuboid from centimetre cubes and count them. A shape 4 long, 3 wide and 2 high holds 24 cubes, so its volume is 24 cm³.

  2. 2

    Spot the layers

    That same cuboid is two identical layers of 12. Seeing it as “area of the base × how many layers” is what turns counting into a formula.

  3. 3

    Use length × width × height

    4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cm³. The order does not matter — multiplication is commutative — so start with whichever pair is easiest.

  4. 4

    Keep every measurement in the same unit

    You cannot multiply 30 cm by 2 m. Convert first: 30 cm × 200 cm, or 0.3 m × 2 m.

  5. 5

    Convert between cm³, ml and litres

    1 cm³ = 1 ml, and 1,000 ml = 1 litre, so 1,000 cm³ = 1 litre. A litre bottle is a 10 cm cube.

Worked examples

Where it usually goes wrong

✕ Multiplying lengths given in different units.

✓ Convert everything to one unit before multiplying. 30 cm × 2 m must become 30 cm × 200 cm.

✕ Writing the answer in cm².

✓ Volume multiplies three lengths, so the unit is cubed. Area is squared; volume is cubed.

✕ Thinking 1 m³ is 100 cm³.

✓ It is 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 cm³. Cube the conversion factor, do not just copy it.

✕ Adding the three dimensions instead of multiplying them.

✓ 8 + 5 + 3 = 16 is not a volume. Volume is always a product.

Why 1 ml equals 1 cm³

It is a definition, not a coincidence. The litre was defined as the volume of a cube 10 cm on each side — that is 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000 cm³. Divide by a thousand and one millilitre is exactly one cubic centimetre.

This makes a useful classroom demonstration: a litre jug of water poured into a 10 cm cube fills it exactly. Children remember the fact far better having seen it.

Volume of compound solids

Year 6 extension work asks for the volume of shapes made from two cuboids stuck together. The approach is the same as compound area: split into cuboids, find each volume, add them. Or box the whole thing in and subtract the gap.

The commonest error is double-counting the overlap, so label each piece's dimensions on the diagram before calculating anything.

Displacement

An irregular object has a volume too, and you measure it by displacement: put it in a measuring cylinder of water and see how far the level rises. A rise of 35 ml means the object's volume is 35 cm³.

This is the practical reason the ml-to-cm³ link matters, and it is a genuinely satisfying experiment for a child who has only ever multiplied three numbers together.

Questions people ask

What is the formula for the volume of a cuboid?
Length × width × height. Equivalently, the area of the base multiplied by the height.
What is the difference between volume and capacity?
Volume is the space a solid occupies; capacity is how much a container can hold. A brick has volume, a jug has capacity.
How many cm³ are in a litre?
1,000, because 1 ml is exactly 1 cm³ and there are 1,000 ml in a litre.
When is volume taught?
Year 5 estimates volume by counting cubes; Year 6 calculates the volume of cuboids using a formula and converts between cubic units.
