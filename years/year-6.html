Year 6 maths: what children learn

Upper Key Stage 2 32 objectives England national curriculum Year 6 worksheet →

Year 6 completes primary maths and ends with the Key Stage 2 SATs in May: one arithmetic paper and two reasoning papers. The content is the broadest of any year, and two entirely new strands appear — ratio and proportion, and algebra.

It is also the year the four operations finally cover fractions and decimals completely, and the year order of operations is formalised as BIDMAS.

What is new since Year 5

The parts of Year 6 that are genuinely new, rather than a continuation of last year's work.

+ Long division

Four-digit numbers divided by a two-digit number, with remainders expressed as whole numbers, fractions or decimals depending on the context. The hardest written method in primary maths.

+ Ratio and proportion

A brand new strand. Relative sizes, scale factors, unequal sharing, and using percentages for comparison. It leans heavily on multiplication and division fluency.

+ Algebra

Also new. Simple formulae, linear sequences, missing-number problems written algebraically, and finding pairs of values that satisfy an equation with two unknowns.

+ Order of operations

BIDMAS, formalised. Brackets, indices, division and multiplication left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right.

+ The mean

The only average assessed at KS2 — calculated and interpreted, including working backwards from a mean to a missing value.

What Year 6 covers

32 objectives, grouped as the national curriculum groups them. Paraphrased for parents — the statutory wording is in the Department for Education programmes of study.

Number and place value

3 objectives
  • Read, write, order and compare numbers up to 10 000 000.
  • Round any whole number to a required degree of accuracy.
  • Use negative numbers in context and calculate intervals across zero.

The four operations

5 objectives
  • Multiply up to four digits by a two-digit number using long multiplication.
  • Divide up to four digits by a two-digit number using long division, interpreting remainders as whole numbers, fractions or by rounding.
  • Identify common factors, common multiples and prime numbers.
  • Use the order of operations to carry out calculations — BIDMAS.
  • Use estimation to check answers and determine accuracy in context.

Fractions, decimals and percentages

8 objectives
  • Simplify fractions using common factors.
  • Compare and order fractions, including fractions greater than 1.
  • Add and subtract fractions with different denominators and mixed numbers.
  • Multiply pairs of proper fractions, giving the answer in its simplest form.
  • Divide proper fractions by whole numbers.
  • Associate a fraction with division to calculate decimal equivalents.
  • Multiply one-digit numbers with up to two decimal places by whole numbers.
  • Recall and use equivalences between simple fractions, decimals and percentages.

Ratio and proportion

4 objectives
  • Solve problems involving the relative sizes of two quantities.
  • Solve problems involving percentages for comparison.
  • Solve problems involving similar shapes where the scale factor is known.
  • Solve problems involving unequal sharing and grouping.

Algebra

5 objectives
  • Use simple formulae.
  • Generate and describe linear number sequences.
  • Express missing number problems algebraically.
  • Find pairs of numbers that satisfy an equation with two unknowns.
  • Enumerate possibilities of combinations of two variables.

Measurement, geometry and statistics

7 objectives
  • Convert between standard units, including miles and kilometres.
  • Calculate the area of parallelograms and triangles, and the volume of cuboids.
  • Draw 2D shapes using given dimensions and angles; build and name 3D shapes from nets.
  • Find unknown angles in triangles, quadrilaterals and regular polygons.
  • Name parts of a circle and know the diameter is twice the radius.
  • Describe positions on the full coordinate grid, all four quadrants.
  • Interpret and construct pie charts and line graphs; calculate and interpret the mean.
Times tables

The tables expectation in Year 6

Tables are assumed completely. In Year 6 they are the engine behind long division, simplifying fractions, finding common denominators, scale factors and factorising — none of which work at speed without them.

A child still counting up to find 7 × 8 in Year 6 will run out of time in the arithmetic paper regardless of how well they understand the methods.

Where Year 6 usually sticks

The problems that come up most often at this stage, and what actually helps with each.

! Long division — everything about it

✓ Write the 1 to 10 multiples of the divisor down the side before starting. It converts the hardest method in primary maths into repeated subtraction and lookup.

! BIDMAS read strictly left to right

✓ Division and multiplication rank equally and are done left to right; the same goes for addition and subtraction. 12 ÷ 2 × 3 is 18, not 2.

! Ratio confused with fractions

✓ In 2 : 3 there are five parts, so the first quantity is 2/5 of the total, not 2/3. Drawing the five parts fixes it.

! Dividing fractions by flipping the wrong one

✓ Keep, change, flip — and it is always the second fraction that flips.

! Reasoning marks lost to misread questions

✓ Underline what is actually being asked before calculating. Most lost reasoning marks in SATs are comprehension, not arithmetic.

Assessment and what to focus on

The Key Stage 2 SATs are taken in May: Paper 1 is 30 minutes of arithmetic, 40 marks; Papers 2 and 3 are 40 minutes each of reasoning, 35 marks apiece. The arithmetic paper rewards fluency directly, and it is the most improvable of the three with regular short practice.

For the reasoning papers, the limiting factor is usually reading rather than maths. Practising the routine — read twice, underline the question, write the number sentence, estimate — earns more marks than another hour of calculation drill. Our word problems guide sets that out in full.

Questions parents ask

What is in the Year 6 SATs maths papers?
Three papers. Paper 1 is arithmetic, 30 minutes and 40 marks. Papers 2 and 3 are reasoning, 40 minutes and 35 marks each. They are taken in May.
What is new in Year 6 maths?
Long division, ratio and proportion, algebra, the order of operations as BIDMAS, the mean, parts of a circle, area of triangles and parallelograms, and volume of cuboids.
How do I help with long division?
Write the first ten multiples of the divisor down the side of the page before starting. It turns the method into subtraction and matching, and removes most of the difficulty.
What is the best way to prepare for the arithmetic paper?
Short, frequent, timed practice. It tests fluency with methods already taught, so speed and accuracy improve measurably with ten minutes a day.
