Year 5 maths: what children learn

Upper Key Stage 2 31 objectives England national curriculum Year 5 worksheet →

Year 5 is the heaviest content year in primary maths. Numbers go to a million, long multiplication and short division arrive, fractions get genuinely difficult, and percentages appear for the first time.

It is also the year that assumes everything before it. Long multiplication is unusable without fluent tables; adding fractions with different denominators is unusable without multiples. Gaps that were survivable in Year 4 become limiting here.

What is new since Year 4

The parts of Year 5 that are genuinely new, rather than a continuation of last year's work.

+ Long multiplication

Four-digit numbers multiplied by a two-digit number, using the formal written method. It is the first algorithm with two layers of partial products and a placeholder zero, and it is unforgiving of shaky tables.

+ Short division

Up to four digits divided by a one-digit number, with remainders interpreted in context. The ‘bus stop’ method, done formally.

+ Percentages

Introduced as ‘parts per hundred’, with the equivalences for 50%, 25%, 20% and 10%. Year 6 does the harder work, but the concept starts here.

+ Fractions with related denominators

Adding and subtracting fractions where one denominator is a multiple of the other, plus mixed numbers and improper fractions, and multiplying proper fractions by whole numbers.

+ Primes, squares and cubes

Prime numbers up to 19 by recall, composite numbers, prime factors, and the notation for square and cube numbers.

What Year 5 covers

31 objectives, grouped as the national curriculum groups them. Paraphrased for parents — the statutory wording is in the Department for Education programmes of study.

Number and place value

5 objectives
  • Read, write, order and compare numbers to at least 1 000 000.
  • Count forwards and backwards in steps of powers of 10.
  • Interpret negative numbers in context.
  • Round any number up to 1 000 000 to the nearest 10, 100, 1000, 10 000 or 100 000.
  • Read Roman numerals to 1000 (M), including years.

Addition and subtraction

3 objectives
  • Add and subtract whole numbers with more than four digits using column methods.
  • Add and subtract numbers mentally with increasingly large numbers.
  • Use rounding to check answers and decide on levels of accuracy.

Multiplication and division

6 objectives
  • Identify multiples, factors, common factors and factor pairs.
  • Know prime numbers up to 19; recognise prime and composite numbers.
  • Multiply numbers up to four digits by a one- or two-digit number using formal written methods, including long multiplication.
  • Divide numbers up to four digits by a one-digit number using short division, interpreting remainders appropriately.
  • Recognise and use square numbers and cube numbers, and the notation ² and ³.
  • Multiply and divide whole numbers and decimals by 10, 100 and 1000.

Fractions, decimals and percentages

7 objectives
  • Compare and order fractions whose denominators are multiples of the same number.
  • Convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions.
  • Add and subtract fractions with denominators that are multiples of the same number.
  • Multiply proper fractions and mixed numbers by whole numbers.
  • Read and write decimal numbers as fractions; recognise thousandths.
  • Round decimals with two decimal places to the nearest whole number and to one decimal place.
  • Recognise the percent symbol and write percentages as fractions and decimals.

Measurement

5 objectives
  • Convert between metric units of length, mass and volume.
  • Know approximate equivalences between metric and imperial units.
  • Measure and calculate the perimeter of composite rectilinear shapes.
  • Calculate the area of rectangles using cm² and m², and estimate the area of irregular shapes.
  • Estimate volume and capacity.

Geometry and statistics

5 objectives
  • Identify 3D shapes from 2D representations.
  • Draw and measure angles in degrees; know angles at a point total 360° and on a straight line 180°.
  • Distinguish regular from irregular polygons.
  • Identify, describe and represent reflections and translations.
  • Complete, read and interpret information in tables, including timetables, and solve problems using line graphs.
Times tables

The tables expectation in Year 5

No new tables — Year 5 assumes all of them. What changes is the use: long multiplication, short division, finding factors and simplifying fractions all require instant recall rather than working out.

If tables are still slow at the start of Year 5, fixing them is the highest-value thing you can do, because it unblocks four separate topics at once.

Where Year 5 usually sticks

The problems that come up most often at this stage, and what actually helps with each.

! Long multiplication without the placeholder zero

✓ The second row is multiplying by tens, so it needs a 0 in the units column. Writing it first, before multiplying, prevents the commonest error in the method.

! Adding fractions by adding both parts

✓ 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. Find a common denominator first. If the denominators have nothing in common, multiply them together.

! Short division stalling on the first digit

✓ When the divisor is bigger than the first digit, carry it across rather than skipping it. 3 into 1 is 0 remainder 1.

! Percentages memorised as a procedure

✓ 10% is a tenth. 25% is a quarter. If a child can only find a percentage by multiplying by 0.01, the understanding is missing.

Assessment and what to focus on

Year 5 is the year to be honest about gaps. The content is demanding enough that a child carrying weak tables or shaky column methods will find it genuinely hard rather than merely challenging, and the fix is almost always further back than the topic being taught.

It is also the last year before SATs preparation starts to shape the timetable, which makes it the best remaining opportunity to build fluency properly rather than revise.

Questions parents ask

What is the hardest topic in Year 5 maths?
Fractions with different denominators, and long multiplication. Both require fluent times tables, so weak recall makes them much harder than they need to be.
Do Year 5 children learn long division?
No — Year 5 covers short division by a one-digit number. Long division by a two-digit number is a Year 6 objective.
What percentages are expected in Year 5?
Understanding percent as parts per hundred, and knowing the equivalences for 50%, 25%, 20% and 10% as fractions and decimals.
Are there tests in Year 5?
No national tests. Most schools run internal assessments, and some use Year 5 results to set groups for Year 6.
