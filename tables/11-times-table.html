The 11 times table

Year 4 12 facts 11 × 11 = 121 Practise this →

The 11 times table is the one children learn fastest, because the first nine facts simply repeat a digit: 11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, 99. It takes about a minute to teach.

Then 11 × 10 = 110 and the pattern appears to break. It has not — it has just become more interesting, and the rule that replaces it works on numbers far bigger than anything in the grid.

Every fact from 11 × 1 to 11 × 12. Read it down the column once, then cover the answers and work back up — recall beats re-reading.

Patterns in the 11 times table

1

Doubled digits up to 9

11 × 1 through 11 × 9 give 11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, 99. The multiplier simply appears twice.

2

11 × 11 = 121 is a palindrome

And so are the powers above it for a while: 11² = 121, 11³ = 1331, 11⁴ = 14641. Those are the rows of Pascal's triangle, which is a genuinely lovely thing to show a curious Year 6.

3

Every multiple is one more lot than ten times

11 × 7 = 70 + 7 = 77. 11 × 12 = 120 + 12 = 132. That is the whole table in one rule, and it works for any number at all: 11 × 46 = 460 + 46 = 506.

The fastest route

Split the digits and drop the sum in the middle

For a two-digit number, separate its digits and put their total between them. 11 × 24: split 2 and 4, add them to get 6, giving 264. 11 × 35 → 3, 8, 5 → 385.

When the middle total reaches 10 or more, carry: 11 × 87 → 8, 15, 7 → carry the 1 into the hundreds → 957. It is a party trick that happens to be real arithmetic — it is exactly what the column method does.

The facts that stick

Not every fact needs the same amount of practice. These are the ones that slow children down in the 11 times table, and what to do about each.

11 × 12 = 132

The only fact in the table that children reliably hesitate on. 120 + 12.

11 × 11 = 121

The square number. Also the first palindrome in the table.

11 × 10 = 110

Easy, but it is where the repeated-digit pattern stops, so it needs saying explicitly.

Division facts

Every multiplication fact is two division facts. A child who knows 11 × 7 but stalls on 77 ÷ 11 has learnt the table in one direction only — which is exactly the gap that shows up later in fractions and simplifying.

121

11 squared

11 × 11 = 121

The square number in this table. It is the area of a square with sides of 11 cm, and the one fact here that reappears in Year 6 work on square and cube numbers and in area questions.

Where the 11 times table turns up

Tables learnt in context are recalled faster than tables learnt as a list. These are the places a child will actually meet multiples of 11.

  • A football team. Eleven players a side, so two teams on the pitch is 22.
  • The 11-plus. Taken in Year 6 in parts of England — and times tables fluency is assumed throughout it.
  • Cricket. Also eleven a side. A touring squad of three elevens is 33.
  • Shoe and clothing sizes. Adult shoe sizes reach 11 and 12, which is where multipack pricing questions come from.

Getting it secure

Because the 11 times table is so quick to learn, it is a good place to show children that a pattern can have a reason. Multiplying by 11 is multiplying by 10 and then adding one more lot, which is why the digit repeats below ten and why the split-and-add trick works above it.

Extending it to two-digit numbers also makes a nice bridge into long multiplication: the middle digit is literally the carry from adding the two partial products. Children who see that connection find the column method far less arbitrary.

Questions people ask

What is the 11 times table?
11, 22, 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, 99, 110, 121, 132 — the multiples of 11 up to 11 × 12.
What is the trick for multiplying by 11?
Split the digits of a two-digit number and put their sum in the middle. 11 × 24 gives 2, 6, 4 — 264. Carry if the middle reaches ten.
Why does the 11 times table repeat digits?
Because 11 × n is 10n + n. Below ten that puts the same digit in the tens and the units column.
When is the 11 times table taught?
Year 4 in England, as part of all tables to 12 × 12.
