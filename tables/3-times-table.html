Times tables · Year 3

The 3 times table

Year 3 12 facts 3 × 3 = 9 Practise this →

The 3 times table is the first real piece of work. There is no doubling shortcut, the answers do not end in a tidy repeating pair of digits, and it arrives in Year 3 alongside the 4 and 8 tables.

It does have one genuinely elegant property, though, and it is worth teaching early: add the digits of any multiple of 3 and the total is also a multiple of 3. That test works for numbers of any size, which makes it far more useful than a memory trick.

Patterns in the 3 times table

1

The digit sum is always 3, 6 or 9

Add the digits of any multiple of 3 and keep going until one digit is left: 12 → 3, 15 → 6, 18 → 9, 21 → 3, 24 → 6, 27 → 9. The cycle 3, 6, 9 repeats forever.

It scales to any number at all. Is 4 731 a multiple of 3? 4 + 7 + 3 + 1 = 15, and 1 + 5 = 6. Yes. No division needed.

2

The last digits use all ten digits

3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30 end in 3, 6, 9, 2, 5, 8, 1, 4, 7, 0. Every digit appears exactly once before the cycle repeats, which is precisely why the 3 times table has no “look at the last digit” shortcut the way 2, 5 and 10 do.

3

Alternate answers are the 6 times table

3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 — every other multiple of 3 is a multiple of 6. Children who know their threes are two-thirds of the way to their sixes.

The fastest route

Double it, then add one more lot

3 × 7: double 7 is 14, add another 7, giving 21. It sounds long-winded written down but it is quick in the head, and it works for every fact in the table.

For the larger ones, go the other way. 3 × 9 = 3 × 10 − 3 = 30 − 3 = 27. Bridging through a ten is usually faster than counting on in threes, which is where most children lose time.

The facts that stick

Not every fact needs the same amount of practice. These are the ones that slow children down in the 3 times table, and what to do about each.

3 × 7 = 21

Nothing about it is memorable, so attach it to something: 21 is three weeks of days.

3 × 8 = 24

24 appears everywhere — hours in a day, two dozen. Also 3 × 8 = 4 × 6, which is a useful cross-check.

3 × 12 = 36

Think 3 × 10 + 3 × 2 = 30 + 6. 36 is also 6 squared, which ties two tables together.

Division facts

Every multiplication fact is two division facts. A child who knows 3 × 7 but stalls on 21 ÷ 3 has learnt the table in one direction only — which is exactly the gap that shows up later in fractions and simplifying.

9

3 squared

3 × 3 = 9

The square number in this table. It is the area of a square with sides of 3 cm, and the one fact here that reappears in Year 6 work on square and cube numbers and in area questions.

Where the 3 times table turns up

Tables learnt in context are recalled faster than tables learnt as a list. These are the places a child will actually meet multiples of 3.

  • Triangles. Three sides, three angles, three vertices. Counting sides across several triangles is × 3 in disguise.
  • Feet in a yard. Three feet to a yard — still used in sport and for fabric.
  • Three-for-two offers. Supermarket pricing is a steady source of real × 3 and ÷ 3 problems.
  • Music. Three beats in a bar of a waltz. Counting bars is multiplying by 3.

Getting it secure

The 3 times table is the one most worth over-practising, because so much later work leans on it. Simplifying fractions depends on spotting that both 9 and 12 divide by 3. Finding equivalent fractions, cancelling ratios, factorising — all of it starts with recognising a multiple of 3 on sight.

The digit-sum test is the single most transferable thing in this table. Teach it as a tool rather than a curiosity: children who can glance at 147 and know instantly that it divides by 3 are doing something genuinely useful for the next ten years of maths.

Questions people ask

What is the 3 times table?
3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36 — the multiples of 3 up to 3 × 12.
How can you tell if a number divides by 3?
Add its digits. If the total is a multiple of 3, so is the number. For 4 731: 4 + 7 + 3 + 1 = 15, which divides by 3, so 4 731 does too.
When is the 3 times table taught?
Year 3 in England, alongside the 4 and 8 times tables.
Why is the 3 times table harder than the 2 or 5?
Its last digits run through all ten digits before repeating, so there is no end-digit pattern to lean on. It has to be learnt as facts, supported by the digit-sum test.

Tables that go with it

Year 4 6 times table Every other multiple of 3 is a multiple of 6. Year 4 9 times table Also has a digit-sum test — the digits of a multiple of 9 add to 9. Year 4 12 times table 12 is 3 × 4, so the two tables share a lot of answers.
