The 5 times table

Year 2 12 facts 5 × 5 = 25 Practise this →

The 5 times table is the friendliest in the set. Every answer ends in 5 or 0, which gives children an instant check on whether an answer is even plausible, and the pattern holds without exception however far you go.

It is also the table with the best real-world anchor: the clock face. Every number on a clock is a multiple of 5 minutes, so a child who can read an analogue clock is practising the 5 times table several times a day without noticing.

Patterns in the 5 times table

1

Every answer ends in 5 or 0

5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 — odd multiplier gives an answer ending in 5, even multiplier gives one ending in 0. That alternation never breaks, so an answer like 23 can be ruled out instantly.

2

It is exactly half of the 10 times table

10 × 7 = 70, so 5 × 7 = 35. Multiply by 10 and halve — often faster than recalling the fact, and it keeps the two tables tied together in a child's head.

3

The clock face is the 5 times table

The 3 on a clock is 15 minutes, the 7 is 35, the 11 is 55. Reading minutes from an analogue clock is pure 5 times table, which is why the two topics are usually taught in the same term.

The fastest route

Multiply by 10, then halve

5 × 8: ten eights are 80, half of 80 is 40. 5 × 14: 140, halve to 70. This scales well past 12, which is why it is worth teaching even to children who already know the table by heart.

It works in reverse for division too. 85 ÷ 5: double 85 to 170, then divide by 10 to get 17. Most adults who are quick at mental arithmetic are doing exactly this.

The facts that stick

Not every fact needs the same amount of practice. These are the ones that slow children down in the 5 times table, and what to do about each.

5 × 7 = 35

The one that hesitates most often. Odd multiplier, so the answer must end in 5 — that narrows it to 35 immediately.

5 × 9 = 45

Half of 90. Or count back one five from 50.

5 × 12 = 60

60 minutes in an hour, which is the most useful fact in the whole table.

Division facts

Every multiplication fact is two division facts. A child who knows 5 × 7 but stalls on 35 ÷ 5 has learnt the table in one direction only — which is exactly the gap that shows up later in fractions and simplifying.

25

5 squared

5 × 5 = 25

The square number in this table. It is the area of a square with sides of 5 cm, and the one fact here that reappears in Year 6 work on square and cube numbers and in area questions.

Where the 5 times table turns up

Tables learnt in context are recalled faster than tables learnt as a list. These are the places a child will actually meet multiples of 5.

  • Minutes on a clock. Every numeral on the dial is five minutes. Twenty-five past is the 5.
  • Fingers and toes. Five on each hand — the oldest counting tool there is, and why base ten exists.
  • 5p coins. Counting 5p coins, and working out how many make a pound, is × 5 and ÷ 5 at once.
  • Tally marks. Tallies are grouped in fives precisely because multiples of five are easy to total.

Getting it secure

Because the 5 times table is so easy, it is the right place to teach what fluency actually means. A child who recites “five, ten, fifteen, twenty…” to reach 5 × 7 does not know the table — they know how to count in fives. Fluency means answering 5 × 7 in isolation, out of order, without the run-up.

Testing out of order is the fix, and it takes about a week. It is also a useful conversation to have with the child: the goal is not to be able to chant the table, it is to have the facts available instantly so that attention can go on the actual problem.

Questions people ask

What is the 5 times table?
5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60 — the multiples of 5 up to 5 × 12.
Why does the 5 times table end in 5 or 0?
Two fives make ten, so every second multiple lands exactly on a ten. The ones in between sit halfway, ending in 5.
What is the quickest way to multiply by 5?
Multiply by 10 and halve the answer. 5 × 14 becomes 140, then 70.
When is the 5 times table taught?
Year 2 in England, alongside the 2 and 10 times tables.
